The Problem

Every estate runs software that no inventory admits exists — internal apps, custom builds, tools deployed outside the standard pipeline. They leave traces in web routes and file paths, but package inventories and CPE fingerprints walk right past them. Unnamed software is unowned software, and unowned software never gets patched, reviewed, or retired.

What It Does

Cavall surfaces software hiding in routes and paths — the evidence normal inventory misses — and lets you name it in plain English. A conversational workflow turns an anonymous route into a tagged, owned custom app: tell Cavall what it is, and the estate finally has a name and a tag for it.

Key Capabilities

  • Route- and path-based discovery — finds software by where it lives and how it's served. Web routes and file paths carry evidence package lists structurally miss.
  • Gap detection against the main inventory — compares what routes and paths reveal against the software and CPE inventories, surfacing exactly what standard discovery walked past.
  • Conversational tagging — describe the mystery app in plain English — "that's our internal billing portal" — and Cavall proposes the tag.
  • Gated writes — every custom-app tag is proposed, human-approved, and logged.

How It Works

Cavall runs against the local navi.db built from your Tenable data — the routes and paths evidence in your findings — and compares what it sees against the standard software inventories. All writes are proposed, human-approved, and logged.

Who It's For

App owners who know what that mystery route is; vulnerability management teams closing the unnamed-software gap in their inventory.

Proof Points

In a reference environment of 268 assets, Cavall hunts the gaps left after Mimir reconciled ~28,700 software rows and ~43,700 CPE rows — the custom and internal apps that appear only as routes and paths. Route and path evidence depends on scan coverage; blind or uncredentialed hosts are called out, not hidden.

Works Better With

Cavall fills gaps for Mimir — custom apps join the unified product map — and for Pythia, catching AI tooling that ships without a recognizable package or CPE.
