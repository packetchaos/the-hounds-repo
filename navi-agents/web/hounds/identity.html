"Every identity — human or not — and its weaknesses."
The two-faced god of doorways — Janus sees human and non-human identities alike, and every door they open.

The Problem

Non-human identities, reused local admins, default credentials, and Active Directory attack paths are the real fuel of lateral movement — and they're invisible to vulnerability scans framed around CVEs. The accounts attackers actually ride through your estate never show up on a severity-sorted finding list.

What It Does

Janus builds a content-first inventory of every identity in the estate — human and non-human — via a 3-pass content sweep. It flags non-expiring, privileged, guest-enabled, and machine identities, then surfaces what attackers love most: account reuse (the same account on many hosts — never assumed to be a shared password), default and blank credentials, AD attack-path exposure, and the coverage gaps (blind hosts) where it can't see at all.

Key Capabilities

  • Identity discovery + classification — a 3-pass content sweep over plugin output finds human and non-human identities alike — service accounts, machine identities, the NHI population no CMDB tracks.
  • Risk flags from identity plugins — non-expiring passwords, privileged and guest-enabled accounts, default/blank credentials, and password-auth SSH, each flagged from specific plugin evidence.
  • Correlation layer — names your crown-jewel identities (privileged accounts on KEV/critical-exposed hosts) and surfaces accounts reused across five or more hosts.
  • Account reuse — the same account name on five or more hosts (scan credentials excluded); a lateral-movement path to check, never assumed to be a shared password.
  • Provenance columns — every identity claim traces back to the plugin that produced it, so IAM teams can verify before acting.
  • Coverage-gap headline — blind and uncredentialed hosts lead the report rather than hiding in a footnote.
  • Gated tagging — identity risk becomes taggable, routable work — proposed and human-approved.

How It Works

Janus runs against the local navi.db built from your Tenable data: the vulns plugin family and output, plus specific identity plugins. All writes are proposed, human-approved, and logged.

Who It's For

IAM and identity-security teams who need visibility beyond the directory; AD admins hunting account reuse and default creds; threat teams tracing the accounts attackers would actually use.

Proof Points

Discovers and classifies identities across a reference environment of 268 mixed RHEL/Windows assets — including the non-human identities no CVE list mentions — correlates privileged identities with KEV/critical exposure to name crown-jewel identities, and flags accounts reused across 5+ hosts. Signals depend on the relevant plugins being present; blind and uncredentialed hosts are called out, not hidden.
