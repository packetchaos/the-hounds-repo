The Problem

Vulnerability lists and identity issues live in separate silos — nobody can see that an internet-exploitable box sits one hop from a crown-jewel server. Teams drown in thousands of findings with no sense of which chain actually gets an attacker to the crown jewels.

What It Does

Fenrir builds an attack graph from the weaknesses Tenable already found: remotely exploitable vulnerabilities, local privilege escalation, credentials shared across hosts, NTLM relay, domain-controller flaws, and failed compliance controls such as LAPS, SMB signing and LLMNR. It finds the most likely routes to every crown jewel (ACR ≥ 7), draws them on an interactive map, and works out the short list of fixes that cuts the most exposure.

Key Capabilities

  • Vulnerability chaining — remote exploit → privilege escalation → credential harvest → lateral movement → crown jewel, each step with its evidence and ATT&CK technique.
  • Compliance as attack enablers — failed CIS / STIG controls (LAPS, SMB signing, LLMNR, WDigest, LSASS, sudo, empty passwords) become the steps they make possible.
  • Credential pivots done honestly — only custom accounts shared across hosts, or local Administrator where LAPS is missing; a common account name alone is not a shared password.
  • Interactive attack map — trace any route, click any host or step for the evidence.
  • Fix first — choke points and a remediation bundle that removes the most crown-jewel exposure, recalculated as each fix is applied.
  • Gated "Attack Path" tags + CSV export — Entry Point, Pivot, Target and Choke Point.

How It Works

Runs against the local navi.db built from your Tenable data: vulns (severity, ports, KEV cross-references, plugin descriptions), compliance results, enumerated accounts, EPSS, and assets (IP, OS, ACR). All writes are proposed, human-approved, and logged.

Who It's For

SOC and threat-led vulnerability teams; red and purple teams validating paths; CISOs who need exposure reporting that shows the chain and how it was broken.

Proof Points

In a 46-asset reference environment with five network segments, Fenrir mapped 35 routes to all 7 crown jewels — chains of up to five steps combining KEV exploits, missing LAPS and shared service accounts — and found 6 fixes that cut crown-jewel exposure by 83%. An isolated lab segment with critical vulnerabilities correctly reached nothing. Reachability is inferred, not observed.

Works Better With

Consumes Laelaps (KEV exploitability), Janus (credential weaknesses, identity pivots), Anubis (crown-jewel ACR), and Charon/Mimir (exploitable and EOL software) — the pack's correlation payoff in one view.

Why It's Different

  • Four signals, one list — exploitability, credentials, criticality, and software risk compound instead of competing.
  • Honest about reachability — paths are inferred from open services, credentials, relay conditions and domain signals, not observed traffic, and every step shows why.
  • Ranked by shared ACR — "crown jewel" means what your business says it means.
  • Gated, reviewable writes — Fenrir proposes tags; a human approves.
