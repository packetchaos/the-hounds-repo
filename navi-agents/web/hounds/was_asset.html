“Map one web app's surface in one view.”
The Beagle, a scent hound that works a trail to map exactly where its quarry has been — here it follows navi's WAS info plugins to lay out one web app's terrain.

The Problem

A WAS scan collects far more than findings: the sitemap the crawler found, the scan's telemetry, cookies, allowed methods, external links, a screenshot. That context is what tells you how an app is built and exposed — and it's buried in informational plugins nobody reads.

What It Does

Point Beagle at a web app and it lays out the terrain from navi's WAS info plugins — sitemap, scan telemetry, target facts, screenshot, external URLs, cookies and allowed methods — plus the app's other informational and security findings. Every plugin opens its raw stored output, proof and request / response headers.

At a glance

Key Capabilities

  • Recon at a glance — sitemap, scan telemetry, target facts, screenshot, external URLs, cookies and allowed methods from WAS info plugins.
  • Raw output on demand — each plugin's stored output, proof and request / response headers.
  • Security findings too — the app's non-informational findings, alongside the other info plugins.
  • Opened from Arachne — a dashboard link (a severity, an OWASP category, a technology, stale scans) narrows the app list to that group.
  • Pick any app — by app or asset UUID, or the riskiest app by default.

How It Works

Beagle reads the WAS apps and findings tables in your local navi.db and changes nothing. WAS data needs a Tenable WAS license and navi config update was.

Who It's For

AppSec engineers and pen testers scoping an app, and app owners who want to see what the scanner saw.

Works Better With

Arachne and Vizsla send you here; Argos is the same deep-dive for infrastructure assets.
